Fishing spots in Owen Sound

Owen Sound's Sydenham River runs through Harrison Park to the Mill Dam fishway and Georgian Bay, with a fall Chinook run that draws crowds. Parts of the river are sanctuaries in spring and in September, so check each stretch.

Rivers around Owen Sound are in Fisheries Management Zone 16; Georgian Bay is Zone 14.

Fishing near Owen Sound through the year

Spring (March to May): spring steelhead in the lower Sydenham River, Sauble River and Bighead River. Trout season opens on most inland stretches on the fourth Saturday in April.

Summer (June to August): the cold water on the Beaver River holds trout through the heat. Fish early and late, and stop when the water passes about 20°C.

Fall (September to November): the Chinook salmon run on the Sydenham River, Sauble River and Bighead River peaks from mid September to mid October, with steelhead following into November.

Winter (December to February): the stretches that are open all year hold steelhead in the slow, deep pools. Mild days after a thaw are best.

Fishing near Owen Sound: good to know

  • Rules change by stretch, often at a road or bridge. Check the official season before you cast.
  • Fish only where access is public or permitted, and respect posted private land.
  • Trout and salmon struggle in warm water. In summer heat, fish early or rest them.

Frequently asked

Where can I go fishing in Owen Sound?

The closest rivers we cover are the Sydenham River (0 km), the Sauble River (27 km) and the Bighead River (28 km). Each guide lists public access and the official season for every stretch.

What fish can I catch near Owen Sound?

Steelhead, Chinook salmon, Brown trout (lake-run), Brown trout, Brook trout and Rainbow trout, depending on the river and the season.

Where can I see the salmon run near Owen Sound?

Owen Sound salmon run on the Sydenham River. The run peaks in September.

What fishing zone is Owen Sound in?

Rivers around Owen Sound are in Fisheries Management Zone 16; Georgian Bay is Zone 14.

Do I need a licence to fish here?

Yes, most anglers need an Outdoors Card and a valid Ontario fishing licence. Ontario residents under 18 or 65 and over have different rules; check the official licensing page.
